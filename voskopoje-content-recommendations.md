```markdown
## Master Painter: David Selenica

### Life & Career

David Selenica (late 17th - mid-18th century) was born in Selenicë, a village in the
Kolonjë region of Albania. He became one of three most prominent figures in medieval
Albanian art, alongside the legendary painters Onufri and Kostandin Shpataraku.

### Artistic Revolution

Selenica revolutionized Post-Byzantine painting through:
- **Bold Color Use**: Pioneered bright, vibrant colors contrasting with traditional somber
  post-Byzantine palettes
- **Fusion of Styles**: Combined Byzantine Paleologan aesthetic with elements of Venetian
  art traditions
- **Artistic Innovation**: Created distinctive portraiture featuring village residents' faces
  on saint bodies (a noted characteristic)
- **Prolific Output**: Painted nearly 1,000 frescoes across multiple churches

### Works Across the Balkans

Selenica's artistic influence extended throughout the Orthodox Christian world:
- **Mount Athos**: Sacred murals for monastic communities
- **Thessaloniki**: Major ecclesiastical commissions
- **Kastoria**: Regional Orthodox churches
- **Voskopojë**: 1722-1726 work on St. Nicholas Church with assistants Constantine and Christos

### Legacy

Selenica founded the distinctive Korçë School of painting, establishing a regional
artistic tradition that influenced Post-Byzantine art for generations. His work is
recognized as masterpieces of the Post-Byzantine style combining emotional depth with
technical mastery.
```

```markdown
## The Zografi Brothers: Post-Byzantine Masters

### Who Were They?

Kostandin Zografi and Athanas Zografi were brothers from Dardhë in the Korçë municipality
of southeastern Albania. Active from 1736-1783, they stand as the most prominent painters
of 18th century post-Byzantine icon art in the entire Balkan region.

### Artistic Style

The Zografi brothers developed a distinctive visual language:
- **Baroque Tendencies**: Linear renderings of religious figures with baroque sensibility
- **Bold Chromatics**: Featured bright white, brilliant blue, and deep red as primary colors
- **Ornamental Richness**: Elaborate decorative elements throughout compositions
- **Technical Mastery**: Sophisticated use of light and shadow for religious instruction

### Their Signature

The brothers consistently signed their works in Greek with the formula:
"By the hands of Konstantinos and Athanasios from Korytsa (Korçë)"
The name "Zografi" literally means "painter" in Greek.

### Work in Voskopojë

The Zografi brothers decorated two major churches in Voskopojë:
- **Church of St. Nicholas**: Outer narthex arcades (painted ~1750)
- **Church of St. Athanasius**: Complete interior decoration (1721-24)

Their lunette painting in St. Athanasius depicting the "Towers of Babylon" falling is
recognized as an exceptional example of their artistic power.

### Broader Influence

Beyond Voskopojë, the brothers' work appears in Orthodox churches and monasteries
throughout central and southern Albania, Mount Athos, Thessaloniki, and the broader
Balkans, making them foundational figures in regional artistic tradition.
```

```markdown
## The Fall of Moscopole: A City's Decline

### Context: Peak of Prosperity (1700-1769)

At its height, Moscopole was:
- A major commercial center trading with Venice, Vienna, and Leipzig
- Home to 24 churches serving a population of 35,000-70,000 residents
- The leading intellectual center of Aromania
- A pioneer of printing and publishing in the Ottoman Balkans
- Nicknamed "New Athens" and "Jerusalem of the Aromanians"

### Catastrophic Events (1769-1788)

#### First Attack: 1769
- **Cause**: Moscopole's residents actively prepared for the Orlov Revolt (supported by
  Russia), making them targets for retribution
- **Actors**: Muslim Albanian bands and Ottoman forces
- **Impact**: Town nearly destroyed; first printing press destroyed
- **Consequence**: New Academy and educational institutions destroyed

#### Continued Raids (1770-1787)
- Series of escalating attacks on the weakened city
- Resident population declined sharply
- Economic collapse as merchants fled
- Church infrastructure severely damaged

#### Final Destruction: 1788
- **Actor**: Troops of Ali Pasha of Ioannina
- **Impact**: Complete razing of remaining structures
- **Outcome**: Moscopole "practically destroyed"

### Aftermath and Diaspora

Survivors of Moscopole's destruction were forced to emigrate:
- **Thessaly**: Major relocation center
- **Macedonia**: Significant Armenian community established
- **Korçë & Berat**: Commercial activities shifted to these nearby cities
- **Global Diaspora**: Aromanian communities spread across Ottoman Empire and beyond

### From Grand City to Small Village

What once housed 35,000-70,000 residents became a small mountain village of under
1,000 people, dependent on small-scale agriculture and herding rather than the great
wool and carpet trades that had made it prosperous.

### Cultural Persistence

Despite physical devastation, Voskopojë maintained its role as the cultural and
linguistic capital of Aromania, preserving the Aromanian language, Orthodox traditions,
and the remarkable artistic legacy of its churches.
```

```markdown
## Voskopojë: Heart of Aramonian Culture

### What is Aramonian?

Aromanian (also called Vlach) is an Eastern Romance language—a linguistic descendant
of Latin spoken in the Balkans. Despite its name, it's more closely related to Romanian
than to Greek, preserving ancient Latin roots in isolated mountain communities.

### Geographic Distribution

Aromanian speakers, known as Aromanians or Vlachs, are traditionally found in:
- Central and southern Albania (primary center)
- Northern Greece
- North Macedonia
- Southern Serbia
- Southwest Bulgaria
- Parts of Romania

### Voskopojë's Role

Voskopojë stands as the most famous and historically significant Aramonian settlement,
earning the poetic designation "Jerusalem of the Aromanians." The village served as:
- Linguistic center preserving Aramonian language
- Cultural capital maintaining Aramonian traditions
- Religious center for Orthodox Aramonian communities
- Educational hub, with Aramonian taught in schools

### Modern Status: An Endangered Heritage

**2018 Language Statistics:**
- Estimated 210,000 native speakers globally
- ~50,000 speakers in Albania (largest concentration)
- ~50,000 in Greece
- ~50,000 in Romania
- Remainder in Serbia, North Macedonia, and Bulgaria

**Endangered Status:** Aramonian is endangered in most regions due to:
- Educational policies favoring majority languages
- Economic migration from traditional villages
- Assimilation pressures in urban areas
- Limited formal documentation and teaching materials

### Voskopojë's Contribution to Scholarship

In 1770, scholars in Voskopojë produced the first multilingual dictionary documenting
four Balkan languages: Greek, Albanian, Aramonian, and Bulgarian. This remarkable
achievement represents an early attempt at linguistic scholarship in the region.

### Living Language

Today, some Voskopojë residents still speak Aramonian, maintaining an unbroken
linguistic connection to ancient Roman populations. Language preservation efforts
are increasingly important as fewer young people learn the language.
```

```markdown
## Winter in Voskopojë: Beyond Summer Tourism

### Winter Season Overview

Voskopojë transforms into a winter destination with unique opportunities. The village's
elevation (1,160m) and location in the Morava Mountains provide excellent conditions for
winter sports and seasonal tourism.

### Climate During Winter

- **Season**: December to March
- **Temperature Range**: -10°C to 5°C (14°F to 41°F)
- **Snow Conditions**: Deep, reliable snowpack from December through February
- **Snowfall**: December averages 106mm, January 85mm
- **Snow Days**: Typically 13-14 snowy/rainy days per month

### Winter Sports Opportunities

#### Skiing & Snowboarding
- **Voskopojë Ski Area**: Local slopes with terrain for multiple skill levels
- **Facilities**: Well-groomed slopes, affordable pricing
- **Advantages**: Less crowded than major European resorts
- **Best Period**: December-February for deepest snow

#### Cross-Country Skiing
- Forest trails through pine forests
- Meadow touring in surrounding highlands
- Minimal infrastructure—suitable for experienced skiers

#### Backcountry & Off-Piste Skiing
- Multiple tour operators offer guided adventures
- Korab Mountain (2,764m) nearby for advanced skiers
- Professional guides available for avalanche risk management
- Season: December-March with avalanche monitoring

#### Snowshoeing
- Forest trails accessible with snowshoes
- Scenic valley walks with mountain views
- Family-friendly winter hiking

### Accommodation in Winter

Most guesthouses and hotels remain open year-round:
- Heat and warm facilities available
- Traditional food and fireplaces
- Some seasonal closures—book in advance
- Winter ambiance appeals to visitors seeking peaceful mountain retreats

### Nearby Korab Mountain

Mount Korab (2,764m) is Albania's highest peak and shared with North Macedonia. Located
near Voskopojë, it offers:
- Challenging ski mountaineering routes
- High-altitude winter climbing
- Advanced backcountry skiing
- Professional guide services available

### Historical Winter Significance

Medieval records indicate Voskopojë's mountain location made winter particularly
challenging during the city's golden age, with snow often cutting off trade routes and
increasing isolation during coldest months.

### Visiting Tips

- Book accommodation in advance (fewer rooms available)
- Check avalanche forecasts before backcountry activities
- Ensure vehicle has winter tires/equipment for mountain roads
- Pack warm layers—temperatures can drop significantly at night
- Some restaurants/shops may have reduced winter hours
- Mobile coverage may be limited in outlying areas during heavy snow
```
